## Methods
Cleaning (Mung-Shu):
- Removed padding spaces on column names and cell values
- Dropped rows with more than 90% missing values

EDA (Boyu):
- Converted `year`, `co2`, `co2_per_capita`, and `gdp` to numeric values
- Focused on five high-income economies with fairly complete post-1990 records: United States, Japan, Germany, South Korea, and United Kingdom
- Compared total emissions over time, per-capita emissions over time, and the latest available year (2024 in this file)

## Results
We use these five countries so the plots stay readable and the comparison is about similar high-income economies, not every country in the dataset.

1. **Total CO₂ over time (1990–2024):** The United States stays far above the others. US emissions rise into the mid-2000s and then decline. The UK and Germany trend downward for most of the period. Japan is relatively flat with a mild decline. South Korea rises for many years and then levels off.
2. **CO₂ per capita over time:** The ranking is not the same as total emissions. The US remains relatively high per person even while falling. South Korea’s per-capita path rises and then plateaus, while the UK and Germany fall to lower levels.
3. **2024 snapshot:** In the latest year in this file, total emissions are roughly US ≫ Japan > South Korea ≈ Germany > UK. Per capita is still led by the US, with South Korea next among this group.

Overall: in this sample, several countries show declining or flat territorial emissions after the 1990s, but per-capita levels still differ a lot.
